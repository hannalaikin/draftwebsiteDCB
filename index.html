Straight Answers

Common Questions & Answers

The questions we hear most from Indianapolis residents — answered directly and honestly.

Question

"Won't my power bill go up because of this?"

Answer

No. In fact, additional power consumed by DC BLOX could put downward pressure on rates by adding more MWh to the revenue requirement used to set rates for all customers. DC BLOX pays for all power at regulated rates and covers 100% of all incremental interconnection costs. AES Indiana has publicly committed to the public utilities commission not to charge regular ratepayers for the costs of supporting increased demand from data centers.

Read the AES Indiana public statement →
Question

"Will these data centers impact our water resources?"

Answer

No. Phase I uses a state-of-the-art DX waterless cooling system — no utility water consumed in normal operations. Phase II's larger buildings use a hybrid air/closed-loop water system that recirculates water internally, requiring no additional utility water once initially primed. No waste water is expelled into the environment.

Question

"Aren't there health risks from living near a data center?"

Answer

No. DC BLOX data centers use no hazardous materials and produce no environmental waste. Diesel generators run only when utility power is unavailable and for brief monthly testing — not enough to impact local air quality. Any electromagnetic fields (EMFs) are reduced by concrete walls and are no greater than those from a typical office building.

Question

"Will this actually help Indianapolis residents?"

Answer

Yes. Substantial tax revenues will help fund schools, police and security, local infrastructure, hospitals, and libraries. Every direct data center job creates approximately 6× that number in indirect local jobs. New fiber networks can also enhance broadband access to underserved communities across Indianapolis.

AES Indiana: Rates Not Expected to Be Negatively Impacted

AES

Official Statement — AES Indiana President

Brandi Davis-Handy, AES Indiana President • September 19, 2025

"We've developed a strategy that shows no negative impact to existing customer rates should AES Indiana power data centers in the future. This is possible because, even though more investments are required to serve those customers, we will be able to spread our costs over a larger amount of electricity sold."

DC BLOX Pays Its Share

DC BLOX covers 100% of all incremental interconnection costs for the additional power needed. No infrastructure cost is passed to existing residential or commercial customers.

Cost-Spreading Benefit

Adding large data center load spreads fixed infrastructure costs across more electricity sold — which can put downward pressure on per-unit rates for all customers.

Public Oversight

AES Indiana's commitments go through the Indiana Utility Regulatory Commission (IURC) — a public process ensuring full transparency and accountability.

Frequently Asked Questions

Questions from Indianapolis residents and neighbors, answered directly. Don't see yours? Reach out — we respond within five business days.

Will my electric or water utility bills increase because of this project?+
No. DC BLOX's rate structure directly covers all incremental interconnection costs for the additional power needed. Adding data center load can actually put downward pressure on rates by adding MWh to the revenue requirement used to set rates for all customers. AES Indiana has publicly committed to the public utilities commission not to charge regular ratepayers for the costs of supporting increased data center demand.

For water: Phase I uses a DX waterless cooling system — no utility water consumed. Phase II's larger buildings use a closed-loop system that recirculates water with no additional utility draw after initial prime. See the AES Indiana public statement.
How does the cooling system work and how much water does it use?+
Phase I (10 MW building): Uses a DX waterless cooling system — no utility water consumed in normal operations whatsoever.

Phase II (20 MW and 48 MW buildings): Uses a hybrid air/closed-loop water system. The system recirculates water internally. Once initially primed, no additional utility water is required and no waste water is expelled into the environment. Far more water-efficient than a typical industrial or warehouse facility on the same site.
Are there any health or environmental risks from living near a data center?+
No. Data centers use no hazardous materials and produce no environmental waste. Diesel backup generators run only when utility power is unavailable and for brief monthly testing — not enough to impact local air quality. Data centers may emit minor electromagnetic fields (EMFs), but concrete walls substantially reduce those emissions. The remaining EMFs are comparable to a typical office building.
Will this increase traffic in the neighborhood?+
No. Data centers are low-traffic operations by nature. A small on-site team works in shifts. There is no large daily workforce commuting, no retail customers, and no constant freight truck activity. Traffic is limited to employees, occasional equipment deliveries, and maintenance — all using designated site access routes.
What about noise from generators and cooling equipment?+
All mechanical equipment is enclosed within buildings. Backup generators run only during utility outages and brief monthly tests — not during normal daily operations. Day-to-day operations are quiet by nature and significantly less disruptive than a typical industrial, warehouse, or distribution facility.
How many jobs will this create for Indianapolis?+
Construction: Hundreds of skilled workers — electricians, HVAC technicians, contractors, and tradespeople — over a multi-year build. DC BLOX prioritizes local contractors wherever possible.

Permanent operations: Data center technicians, network engineers, facilities managers, security, and support staff — specialized positions paying wages well above county averages.

Indirect jobs: Every direct data center job creates approximately 6 additional jobs in the local economy. (Source: PwC)
How does this project benefit Indianapolis residents specifically?+
Substantial tax revenues reduce the burden on residents and fund better schools, police and security, local infrastructure, hospitals, and libraries. Local employment — direct and indirect — keeps wages and spending in Indianapolis. New fiber connectivity built to support the campus can improve broadband access for businesses and underserved communities. Long-term, this is a stable, recession-resistant addition to the local tax base for decades.
Is DC BLOX a credible, long-term partner for Indianapolis?+
Yes. DC BLOX was founded in 2014 and has been operating data centers across the Southeast for over a decade. The company has raised over $1.4 billion in institutional capital backed by Bain Capital and Post Road Group, and has a demonstrated track record of community investment in every market it enters. Data centers are permanent infrastructure — physically anchored to their site. This is a multi-decade commitment to Indianapolis. Learn more at dcblox.com →
