Straight Answers

Common Questions & Answers

The questions we hear most from Indianapolis residents — answered directly and honestly.

Question

"Won't my power bill go up because of this?"

Answer

No. In fact, additional power consumed by DC BLOX could put downward pressure on rates by adding more MWh to the revenue requirement used to set rates for all customers. DC BLOX pays for all the power it uses at regulated rates and covers 100% of all incremental direct interconnection costs for the additional power needed. AES Indiana has also publicly committed to the public utilities commission not to charge regular ratepayers for the costs of supporting increased demand from data centers.

Read the AES Indiana public statement →
Question

"Will these data centers impact our water resources?"

Answer

No. Phase I is built with a state-of-the-art DX waterless cooling system — no utility water is consumed during normal operations. For the larger Phase II buildings, a hybrid air/closed-loop water system recirculates water internally, requiring no additional utility water once initially primed. No waste water is expelled into the environment.

Question

"Aren't there health risks from living near a data center?"

Answer

No. DC BLOX data centers use no hazardous materials and produce no environmental waste. Diesel generators run only when utility power is unavailable and for monthly testing — not enough to impact local air quality. Data centers may emit minor electromagnetic fields (EMFs), but concrete walls substantially reduce emissions. The remaining EMFs are no greater than those from a typical office building.

Question

"Will this actually help Indianapolis residents?"

Answer

Yes. Substantial tax revenues will help reduce the burden on residents and can improve schools, police and security, local infrastructure, hospitals, and libraries. Every direct data center job creates approximately 6× that number in indirect jobs for the local economy. New local fiber networks can also enhance broadband access to underserved communities across Indianapolis.

What is a Data Center?

A data center is the physical building that houses the computers and software powering everything from streaming services and online banking to healthcare systems, remote work tools, and artificial intelligence. Think of it as essential infrastructure — like a power plant or water treatment facility — that keeps modern life running reliably.

Why are more data centers needed now?

The growth of AI, cloud computing, streaming, and the Internet of Things is driving massive demand for new data infrastructure located closer to where people live and work. Hyperscalers including AWS, Microsoft, Google, Meta, and Oracle are all investing heavily in expanding their capacity.

What everyday services depend on data centers?

Communications, entertainment, social media, online learning, business productivity (Teams, Slack, Zoom), enterprise systems, payment networks, digital wallets, healthcare records, transportation systems, and emergency services all depend on data center infrastructure.

Frequently Asked Questions

Questions from Indianapolis residents and neighbors, answered directly. Don't see yours? Reach out — we respond within five business days.

Will my electric or water utility bills increase because of this project?+
No. DC BLOX's rate structure directly covers all incremental interconnection costs for the additional power needed. Adding data center load can actually put downward pressure on rates by adding MWh to the revenue requirement used to set rates for all customers. AES Indiana has publicly committed to the public utilities commission not to charge regular ratepayers for the costs of supporting increased demand from data centers.

For water: Phase I uses a DX waterless cooling system — no utility water consumed in normal operations. Phase II's larger buildings use a closed-loop system that recirculates water with no additional utility draw after initial prime. See the AES Indiana public statement.
How does the cooling system work and how much water does it use?+
Phase I (10 MW building): Uses a state-of-the-art DX waterless cooling system. No utility water is consumed during normal operations.

Phase II (20 MW and 48 MW buildings): Uses a hybrid air/closed-loop water system. The system recirculates water internally — once initially primed, no additional utility water is required and no waste water is expelled into the environment. This is significantly more water-efficient than a typical industrial or warehouse facility.
Are there any health or environmental risks from living near a data center?+
No. Data centers use no hazardous materials and produce no environmental waste. Diesel backup generators run only when utility power is unavailable and for brief monthly testing — not enough to impact local air quality. Data centers may emit minor electromagnetic fields (EMFs), but concrete walls substantially reduce those emissions. The remaining EMFs are comparable to a typical office building.
Will this increase traffic in the neighborhood?+
No. Data centers are low-traffic operations by nature. A small on-site team works in shifts. There is no large daily workforce commuting, no retail customers, and no constant freight truck activity. Traffic is limited to employees, occasional equipment deliveries, and maintenance personnel — all using designated site access routes.
What about noise from generators and cooling equipment?+
All mechanical equipment is enclosed within the buildings. Backup generators run only during utility outages and brief monthly tests — not during normal daily operations. Day-to-day operations are quiet by nature and are designed to be significantly less disruptive than a typical industrial, warehouse, or distribution facility on the same site.
How many jobs will this create for Indianapolis?+
Construction: Projects like DC BLOX Indianapolis require hundreds of skilled workers — electricians, HVAC technicians, general contractors, and other tradespeople — over a multi-year build period. DC BLOX prioritizes local contractors and labor wherever possible.

Permanent operations: Data center technicians, network engineers, facilities managers, security, and support staff — specialized positions paying wages well above county averages.

Indirect jobs: Every direct data center job creates approximately 6 additional jobs in the local economy across telecommunications, maintenance, services, and the businesses that support employees and operations.
How does this project benefit Indianapolis residents specifically?+
Substantial tax revenues reduce the burden on residents and fund better schools, police and security, local infrastructure, hospitals, and libraries. Local employment — direct and indirect — keeps wages and spending in Indianapolis. New fiber connectivity built to support the campus can improve broadband access for commercial businesses and underserved communities. Long-term, a large permanent infrastructure investment provides a stable, recession-resistant addition to the local tax base for decades.
Is DC BLOX a credible, long-term partner for Indianapolis?+
Yes. DC BLOX was founded in 2014 and has been operating data centers in Southeast communities for over a decade. The company has raised over $1.4 billion in institutional capital, is backed by Bain Capital and Post Road Group, and holds a demonstrated track record of community investment — through sponsorships, volunteering, education programs, and active participation in local life. Data centers are permanent infrastructure investments physically anchored to their site. This is a multi-decade commitment to Indianapolis.
